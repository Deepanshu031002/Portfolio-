Windows 11 — Fixing the Feature a Billion People Touch Every Day

Final assessment project · 2026 · Research, UX design, and a functioning click-through prototype

The Problem

Windows 11 has crossed 1 billion active users and holds about 66.6% of the desktop OS market. At that scale, you don't need more features, you need the one feature everybody actually uses to work well. I picked Windows Search because it shows up in almost every review as the most-used and most-criticized part of the OS. It's slow, cluttered, and it's gotten harder than it should be to just find a file.

Windows — 66.6%
macOS — 11.0%
Linux — 4.3%
ChromeOS — 2.2%

Desktop OS market share, 2026. The jump from ~50% to ~73% of the Windows install base is migration-driven — Windows 10's October 2025 end-of-life — not organic demand.

Key Insight

Windows doesn't win by being the best at any one thing. macOS wins on creative work, ChromeOS on simplicity, Linux on customization. Windows wins by being good enough at everything, at a scale nobody else touches, plus a feedback loop with users (the Insider Program) that even its critics respect. That's exactly why Search matters so much here. A small fix compounds across more than a billion people, and around 500 million PCs can't even upgrade because of the TPM 2.0 requirement, so what's already installed has to keep working well.

What I Recommended

  1. Redesign Windows Search — highest visibility, most defensible given the evidence. The flagship recommendation, detailed below.
  2. Make AI features opt-in by default — rebuild trust incrementally after the Copilot and Recall backlash, rather than pushing AI where it isn't wanted.
  3. Expand Windows 365 Cloud PC messaging — reach the ~500M hardware-stranded users without requiring new hardware.
  4. Continue investing in File Explorer reliability — a quieter, less visible trust-builder, but a genuine long-standing complaint.

Goals, Personas & Information Architecture

The Everyday Multitasker
  • Non-technical, juggles apps and files daily
  • Wants search to "just work," gets overwhelmed by clutter
  • "I just want to find my file — not learn a new tool."
The Power User
  • Keyboard-driven, wants filters and precise control
  • Notices and is frustrated by every extra click or delay
  • "Everything else is instant. Why is Search the exception?"

Information architecture: a single search entry point leads to tabbed, categorized results (All / Apps / Documents / Web / Settings), with a persistent "best match" anchor at the top of every query. Initial wireframes tested 5 tabs — a simulated stakeholder review flagged one too many, so "People" was folded into a broader "Contacts" filter within Apps. IRCTC Rail Connect — Product Teardown

Tools: User research, competitive analysis, financial analysis, business-context assessment

The Problem

IRCTC Rail Connect is India's official train booking platform. By law, it's the only one allowed to issue Indian Railways e-tickets, which gives it around 73% of the online rail booking market. But having the only license doesn't mean it has the best experience. Partners like ConfirmTkt and ixigo Trains run on the same IRCTC backend and still manage to win people over on UX, especially during Tatkal booking, which is the moment that matters most to users.

I'm an active user of both Tatkal and advance booking, so I wanted to actually dig into where the product breaks down, and what a Product Manager would recommend fixing first.

How I Approached It

  1. Firsthand product use — documented specific friction points across Tatkal booking, regular booking, and PNR status myself, instead of going off secondhand reviews.
  2. Market and competitive research — sized the industry, mapped competitors, pulled real financial numbers from IRCTC's quarterly results.
  3. User personas — built around booking behavior (Tatkal urgency, advance planning, occasional use) rather than demographics.
  4. Priority framing — sequenced recommendations by friction solved versus effort required.
  5. Business context — looked at where IRCTC sits in its product lifecycle and its current reinvestment phase.

Key Insight

The most interesting finding here wasn't really a UX bug, it was more structural. IRCTC's most profitable segment is also the one with the worst experience. Internet Ticketing runs at an 85% EBITDA margin, the highest of any business line, but it's also where people hit the most friction: Tatkal crashes, CAPTCHA failures, seats that show as available and then vanish. Indian Railways had already started a 5x-capacity backend upgrade around the same time, which lines up almost exactly with the failures I ran into myself.

What I Recommended

Strategy & Roadmap
  • Pair the backend upgrade with a visible UX fix-list
  • Treat Tatkal as its own product with dedicated reliability targets
  • Sequence UX investment behind the current reinvestment window
Product & UX
  • Save passenger details for future bookings
  • Sync seat availability in real time
  • Show passenger name in PNR for counter-booked tickets
  • Graceful payment-redirect fallback
Marketing & Trust
  • Promote the new booking system transparently
  • Lean into official-authority and scale messaging
  • Publish a visible product roadmap

The idea behind this order is simple: fix the highest-friction, lowest-effort gaps first, since those reuse data and infrastructure IRCTC already has.

What I'd Measure

  • Tatkal booking completion rate within the critical first 3 minutes
  • Drop-off rate during the Tatkal-vs-advance-booking lockout window
  • Repeat-booking time (impact of saved passenger lists)
  • Support/complaint volume tied to seat-availability mismatches

CRM Sales Performance Dashboard

Tools: Requirements definition, role/UX design, Claude (AI-assisted frontend build)

The Problem

Sales teams have different roles — admins, team leads, consultants — but they usually end up staring at the same raw data with no easy way to see it the way they actually need to. Most CRMs either force one generic view on everyone or take a lot of engineering effort to build separate ones. I wanted to design and prototype something lighter: a dashboard that gives each role exactly the view they need, nothing more.

What I Designed

Lead Tracking

A clear pipeline view so reps and leads see where every lead stands

Payment Verification

A structured way to confirm and track payment status against closed leads

Role-Based Views

Distinct dashboards for Admin, Team Lead, and Consultant

How I Approached It

  1. Defined the roles first — mapped what each role needs to see and act on, starting from the workflow, not the UI.
  2. Designed the information architecture — how leads flow from creation through payment verification.
  3. Directed an AI-assisted build — used Claude to build the full frontend prototype across several iterations.
  4. Stress-tested the flows — walked each role's journey end-to-end, not just the happy path.
View Live Prototype

What I'd Measure If This Went Live

  • Time from lead creation to payment verification, by role
  • % of leads stalled at the verification step, and where
  • Admin time spent per week on manual oversight
  • Consultant adoption of self-serve dashboards

Worth saying plainly: I directed and reviewed this end-to-end. The role definitions, workflow logic, and design decisions are mine — Claude helped build the actual frontend prototype.

Guesstimate: Traffic Lights in Delhi

Tools: Structured estimation, logical framing, Google Slides

The Problem

A classic guesstimate question you'll hear in PM interviews: how many traffic lights are there in Delhi? There's no dataset for this one. The whole point is showing how you think when the facts just aren't available.

How I Approached It

  1. Defined what counts as "a traffic light" — individual vehicle signal heads at intersections, excluding pedestrian and railway signals.
  2. Grounded it in Delhi's scale — ~3.5 crore people, a huge road network.
  3. Estimated signalized intersections — about 3,000 across the city.
  4. Split by size: Major 20% → 600, Medium 50% → 1,500, Small 30% → 900.
  5. Assigned lights per type: Major ~15, Medium ~8, Small ~4.

The Calculation

CategoryIntersectionsLights EachSubtotal
Major Junctions600159,000
Medium Junctions1,500812,000
Small Junctions90043,600
Total: 9,000 + 12,000 + 3,600 = 24,600, rounded to a working range of 24,000–25,000 traffic lights.

What This Was Really About

  • Break it down first — impossible to answer directly at full size
  • Make assumptions you can defend — every number is a judgment call grounded in something real
  • Segment instead of averaging — far more honest than treating every intersection the same
